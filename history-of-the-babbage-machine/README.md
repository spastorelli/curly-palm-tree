# Introduction

## Introduction

In the first half of the 19th century, the English mathematician **Charles Babbage** conceived a series of mechanical computing machines that anticipated the general-purpose computer by more than a hundred years. Built from brass, steel, and thousands of precisely machined gears, his engines were designed to calculate and print mathematical tables automatically — removing the human errors that plagued the hand-computed tables of the age.

Babbage never saw his most ambitious designs completed in his lifetime, yet the ideas behind them — automatic calculation, a separation of memory and processing, and control by punched cards — are recognisably the foundations of modern computing.

This site traces the story of Babbage's machines: the man behind them, the Difference Engine, the visionary Analytical Engine, the contribution of Ada Lovelace, and the machines' rediscovery and reconstruction in the modern era.
