# Timeline

## Timeline

This **timeline** traces the key events of Charles Babbage's life and work, from his birth in 1791 to the modern reconstructions that proved his designs could work.

### Early life (1791–1821)

* **1791** — Charles Babbage is born in London on 26 December.
* **1810** — Enters Trinity College, Cambridge.
* **1812** — Co-founds the **Analytical Society** with John Herschel and George Peacock.
* **1814** — Graduates from Peterhouse, Cambridge, and marries Georgiana Whitmore.
* **1816** — Elected a Fellow of the **Royal Society**.
* **1820** — Helps found the Astronomical Society, later the Royal Astronomical Society.
* **1821** — Checking error-filled tables with Herschel, wishes the calculations "could be executed by steam".

### The Difference Engine years (1822–1833)

* **1822** — Proposes the **Difference Engine** and builds a small working model.
* **1823** — Secures government funding; engineer **Joseph Clement** begins construction.
* **1826** — Publishes his system of **mechanical notation**.
* **1827** — His wife Georgiana dies; Babbage travels on the Continent for over a year.
* **1828** — Appointed **Lucasian Professor of Mathematics** at Cambridge, a post he holds until 1839.
* **1832** — Completes the demonstration portion of Difference Engine No. 1 and publishes _On the Economy of Machinery and Manufactures_.
* **1833** — Work stops after the dispute with Clement (see [The Difference Engine](the-difference-engine.md)).

### The Analytical Engine (1834–1849)

* **1834** — Begins designing the **Analytical Engine**, a general-purpose, programmable machine. Helps found the Statistical Society of London.
* **1838–39** — Builds a **dynamometer car** to study the Great Western Railway (see [Babbage's Other Inventions](babbages-other-inventions.md)).
* **1840** — Presents the Analytical Engine at a scientific meeting in **Turin**.
* **1842** — Luigi Menabrea publishes an account of the Turin lecture. The government formally abandons the Difference Engine.
* **1843** — **Ada Lovelace** publishes her translation with her Notes, including Note G (see [Ada Lovelace and the First Program](ada-lovelace-and-the-first-program.md)).
* **1847** — Builds an early **ophthalmoscope**.
* **1847–49** — Designs **Difference Engine No. 2**.

### Later years (1850–1871)

* **1851** — Proposes **occulting lights** for lighthouses.
* **1850s** — Breaks the **Vigenère autokey cipher**.
* **1864** — Publishes his autobiography, _Passages from the Life of a Philosopher_.
* **1871** — Dies in London on 18 October, aged 79.

### After Babbage (1878–present)

* **1878** — A British Association committee recommends against building the Analytical Engine.
* **1910** — Babbage's son Henry completes a section of the Analytical Engine Mill.
* **1991** — The **Science Museum** in London completes the calculating section of Difference Engine No. 2 for the bicentenary of Babbage's birth.
* **2002** — The engine's printing and stereotyping apparatus is finished (see [Legacy and Reconstructions](legacy-and-reconstructions.md)).

{% hint style="info" %}
Many of Babbage's ideas were rediscovered independently in the twentieth century. Pioneers of electronic computing in the 1940s were often surprised to find how closely their designs echoed his century-old plans.
{% endhint %}
